Max was a physicist genius who's extremely dedicated to his work. He had always felt a gaping hole in his heart that cannot be filled, so he drowned himself in his work as a distraction, but he just sinks deeper and deeper.

On his 25th birthday, a terrifying discovery was made. For unknown reasons, a blackhole was heading to Earth at tremendous speed. The best physicists were gathered together for one singular task: kill the blackhole and save humanity. Max was selected as the project lead, and he sunk himself into the deep end straight away. The perfect distraction.
But will killing this blackhole also kill the void in his heart?

# Ideas
- Fly lots of people to orbit the blackhole.
- Inevitable failure. But it is okay.
- Max is afraid of drowning himself in fantasy to relieve himself from sadness, as he saw it as running away. He feels that if he stops feeling sad about his mother's death, it would reduce her importance in his life.
- Max's mother was an astronomer who led the blackhole imaging project. She passed away right after the image finished rendering.
	- From that point, Max associated the blackhole with his mother's death.
- The brighter the star, the larger the void it will leave once it dies. Just like how the more important someone is to us, the more painful their passing will be. But it is their legacy. It is how we can feel them. So don't run away from it. Embrace it. And live with it.

The line on the monitor stopped dancing and became flat. Father and other relatives yelled and cried. Tear covered their faces. I cried too. I wasn't sure why, but I knew that she won't wake up anymore. I hugged her close and shook her, but she just laid there. Peacefully.

[20 years later]

I rushed to the hospital. It felt all so familiar. The image of the black hole and my mother filled my mind, and I couldn't shake them away. I tried telling myself that Ann would be okay, she would be fine. The black hole won't shatter my world farther than it would have. Literally.

The black hole was speeding towards us at tremendous speed, it was going to tear through our solar system and destroy everything, so why take more away from now? Why not let me enjoy my last moments fully?

"Ann!" I swung open the door and yelled. She laid there. Peacefully still. The line on the heart monitor flat. I collapsed on the floor and cried. I cannot take it anymore.

"What are you doing?" A sweet voice asked.

"Huh? Ann, you're okay?"

"Yeah I'm fine. Oh, looks like the doctor forgot to turn off the heart rate monitor. I was so tired I must've slept completely through it."

"Oh thank god!" I held her close. Her body warm, and her heart beating energetically. I felt a breeze on my cheeks. The curtains danced gently. The sky was bright and cloudless. Cars honked at each other and people chattered about. It was a beautiful day.

Everything was so normal. I was so happy that it was this way. But soon, a black hole would rip through the sky and devour everything. Strangely, that did not bothered even in the slightest. It seemed that Ann's passing would bring me far greater despair than any world-ending event.

I looked at her. She smiled so bright. It did not feel like we were only a couple of years from destruction. I think, if I could hold her in my arms, it did not matter if a black hole was coming or not. I could live with that...yes I could.

Live with it...It suddenly occurred to me that we did not need to destroy the black hole. We need to live with it.
